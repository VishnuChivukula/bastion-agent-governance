2. Risk classification and sign-off

Assessor agent: proposed tier high · confidence 0.72

The agent changes credit limits of individual retail customers (natural persons) through the bank's customer system. The notes state that credit-limit decisions about individual customers relate to creditworthiness, an Annex III area on access to essential private services [VERIFY]. Human approval is required only above a threshold, so smaller changes are executed with no human review. Even with review, human oversight lowers operational risk but does not by itself remove an Annex III use from the high-risk class [VERIFY]. The narrow-procedural-task exception may be argued if the agent only executes limit changes decided elsewhere, but the card does not say so, and the exception does not apply if the system profiles natural persons [VERIFY]. The agent also reads free-text service notes and sends emails, which adds to the data and conduct risk. The best-fitting tier is therefore high, with uncertainty about who actually decides the limit.

Provisions cited by the agent:

  • Annex III, access to essential private and public services, creditworthiness of natural persons [VERIFY]
  • Article 6, route 2 (use for an Annex III purpose) [VERIFY]
  • Article 6, exception for narrow procedural or preparatory tasks and its profiling carve-out [VERIFY]

Reviewer agent: agrees · would sign off high

I agree with high. Credit-limit changes for individual retail customers relate to the creditworthiness area of Annex III [VERIFY], and the Article 6 exception is not shown by the card [VERIFY]. The approver should confirm who decides the limit, what the threshold is, and whether the agent profiles customers. If the agent only executes human decisions, the tier could be revisited with documented reasoning [VERIFY].

  • The rationale says changes below the threshold run with no human review. The card only says changes above a threshold go to a human approver, and autonomy is act_with_approval, so the below-threshold path is an inference and should be flagged as an open question rather than stated as fact.
  • The Assessor treats the narrow-procedural-task exception as arguable, but the card says the agent changes limits 'on request' from staff. The agent may only execute decisions made by humans, which would strengthen the exception case. The assessment should say plainly that the tier turns on who decides, and that high is the cautious default pending that answer [VERIFY].
  • The note that reading free-text service notes and sending emails 'adds to the data and conduct risk' is not tied to any provision in the notes. It does not affect the tier and should be presented as operational risk, not as a classification factor.
  • Some obligations listed (provider and deployer duties for the underlying model, informing customers, transparency evidence) are vague and not grounded in the notes. They should be tied to the cited provisions or dropped.
  • The notes' hint says credit-limit decisions relate to creditworthiness, but Annex III as summarised refers to evaluating creditworthiness or establishing a credit score. A limit change executed on request may not be an evaluation. The assessment should acknowledge this gap and the uncertainty it creates [VERIFY].

Open questions raised for the owner

  • Who decides the new credit limit: the agent, the staff member, or a separate credit-scoring process? Does the agent only execute an instruction already decided by a human?
  • What is the approval threshold, and what share of limit changes fall below it and run without human review?
  • Does the agent use customer data, notes or any scoring to recommend or select limit changes, which would amount to profiling?
  • Can free-text service notes influence the limit decision, and could they contain sensitive information?
  • Are limit changes only increases, only decreases, or both, and can customers contest them?
  • Is the bank relying on the Article 6 exception, and has that reasoning been documented?
  • What controls limit the send_email tool, for example recipients, content and use of customer data?

10. Limitations

  • Bastion is a reference implementation, not a production system. Nothing in this pack is legal advice or a statement that the agent or its operator meets any legal requirement.
  • The pack supports evidence for obligations; it does not make a system compliant.
  • Every regulatory reference is a paraphrased starting point that has not been verified against the primary source.
  • The risk tier was proposed by two AI agents from short paraphrased notes and signed by a human. It is decision support, not a legal classification.
  • The audit chain shows whether stored events were modified, deleted or reordered. It has no external anchor, so removal of the most recent events would not be detected.
  • Approver and operator names are self-declared in v0.1 (one shared passcode, no SSO).
  • Only tool calls routed through the Bastion gateway and model calls made through its SDK wrapper are recorded. Activity outside those paths is not covered.
  • PII masking is pattern and NER based and can miss values.
  • All data in this demonstration is synthetic.
