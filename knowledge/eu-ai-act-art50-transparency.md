# EU AI Act, Article 50: transparency obligations [VERIFY]

Paraphrased summary; not the legal text. Tier in Bastion: `limited` (transparency duties, no high-risk regime).

For certain systems that are not high-risk, the main duty is to tell people what they are dealing with. In outline [VERIFY]:
- People interacting with an AI system (for example a chatbot) are informed that it is an AI system, unless that is obvious from the context.
- Synthetic audio, image, video or text output is marked as artificially generated in a machine-readable way.
- Deployers of emotion recognition or biometric categorisation systems (where permitted) inform the people exposed to them.
- Deployers disclose that deepfake content has been artificially generated or manipulated; a lighter duty applies to evidently artistic or satirical work.

Systems with no specific obligation under the Act (for example spam filters, internal drafting aids with human review, developer tooling) are treated as `minimal` in Bastion [VERIFY].

Assessment hints: a customer-facing assistant that only answers questions and takes no decisions about the person is typically a transparency case. Generating synthetic likenesses of real people is the deepfake case.
